About

The Constitution of India is the supreme law of the country. It was adopted on 26 November 1949 and came into effect on 26 January 1950, which is celebrated as Republic Day every year. It was drafted by the Constituent Assembly after extensive discussions and debates. The father of constitution Dr. B.R. Ambedkar, the Chairman of the Drafting Committee, is known as the Father of the Indian Constitution for his significant contributions. The Constitution defines the powers and responsibilities of the government and guarantees the rights of citizens. It establishes India as a sovereign, socialist, secular, democratic republic. The Constitution provides Fundamental Rights to ensure equality, freedom, and justice for all citizens. It also includes Fundamental Duties that encourage citizens to contribute to the nation's development. The Directive Principles of State Policy guide the government in promoting social and economic welfare. The Constitution establishes an independent judiciary to uphold justice and protect constitutional values. It ensures a federal system of governance with powers shared between the Union and the States. Originally, it contained 395 Articles, 22 Parts, and 8 Schedules. Today, it has been amended several times to meet the changing needs of society. The Indian Constitution remains one of the longest written constitutions in the world and serves as the foundation of India's democracy and unity.

DR.AMBEDKAR

Bhimrao Ramji Ambedkar was born on 14 April 1891 in Mhow, Madhya Pradesh. He was a distinguished scholar, jurist, economist, and social reformer who dedicated his life to promoting equality and social justice. As the Chairman of the Drafting Committee of the Constituent Assembly, he played a leading role in framing the Constitution of India. He strongly advocated for Fundamental Rights, equality before the law, and the abolition of social discrimination. Dr. Ambedkar earned degrees from prestigious institutions abroad and made significant contributions to economics, law, and political thought. He also served as India's first Law Minister after independence. His vision helped establish democratic values and constitutional principles in India. He is respectfully remembered as the Father of the Indian Constitution, and his contributions continue to inspire generations.
